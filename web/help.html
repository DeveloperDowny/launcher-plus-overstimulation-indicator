Help, notes & assumptions

TL;DR

This app estimates how overstimulated you are by adding up what you launch. Every app or activity has a weight (usually 1–10) reflecting how strongly it tends to overstimulate a highly sensitive person, loosely following Elaine Aron's The Highly Sensitive Person. Each launch adds points; time passing and breaks bring the level down.

Tap Set my level any time to say how you really feel, and the app re-anchors to your number. Bands: calm 0–25, steady 26–50, loaded 51–75, frayed 76–100.

It only knows what you launch here, not what you do inside an app, so treat the number as a rough estimate, not medical advice. Everything you tap is logged under your tester ID so the creator can learn from it. Please don't put personal details in feedback.

How to use it

  • Launch: tap an app's main button to open its website in a new tab and add its points. Smaller buttons pick a specific mode (for example Instagram → reels, or YouTube → shorts).
  • Set my level: move the slider to how overstimulated you feel right now (0–100) and tap the button (it stays greyed out until you move the slider). Your level jumps to that number. If that's 30 or more points away from the app's estimate, you'll be asked to confirm first. Afterwards you'll be asked if the app's estimate was close.
  • Take a break: tap when you step away from your phone, and tap End break when you're back (opening an app or setting your level also ends it). The longer the break, the more it helps, up to 30 minutes: counts up to 30 min. Full effect at 15 min.
  • How do you feel right now? Now and then (at most 4 times a day, at least 90 minutes apart) the app asks how you feel: after a long rest, the first time a burst gets high, and at random. Answer honestly or skip; skipping changes nothing. These answers show whether the number tracks how you actually feel.
  • How long were you on…? If you come back to the launcher more than 20 minutes after opening an app, it asks how long you were actually in it, because it can't tell whether you put your phone down. If you don't answer, it assumes 20 minutes, or 45 for streaming apps (Netflix, Prime Video, Hotstar and the like), since a show or film usually runs longer.
  • Good morning card: after a long gap (5+ hours, usually sleep) you'll be asked how you slept and how heavy the day looks. Once a day; skip it if you like.
  • Right now I… chips (ate a meal, had caffeine, am with people, am stressed and so on): one tap notes what's going on. They never change your level; they help the analysis.
  • Feedback: use the box at the bottom of the main page. It's the most useful thing you can give.

How the level is calculated

  • Launch: adds weight × 1.5 points (a weight-10 activity adds 15%). That covers the first 5 minutes in the app.
  • Time in an app: every launch is timed, including ones that open nothing (meals, walks, 7 Habits, the roulette): the time between starting it and coming back to the launcher, or tapping something else, counts. "Going to sleep" isn't timed; waking up covers that. After the first 5 minutes, each extra minute adds weight × 0.1 points (12 minutes of shorts adds 7 more), and the level doesn't drop while you're still in a stimulating app. Time in a calming app lowers it instead.
  • Calming activities (breathing, meditation, reading a physical book, calm music, nature sounds, journaling, stretching and similar) have negative weights, so launching them lowers the level instead. They never count towards app-switching. Calming isn't the same for everyone: if one of them feels like too much, skip it.
  • Neutral launches (sleep, back to work, meals, Bhagwat Gita a Day, 7-habits, roulette) have weight 0: they are logged but don't change the level. Roulette and 7-habits open their site; the rest are dummies that open nothing.
  • Lots of overstimulation: when a launch or "Set my level" takes you into the frayed band (above 75), a list of calming activities is copied to your clipboard and the roulette site opens so you can spin for one. If your browser blocks the extra tab, use the red roulette button on your level card.
  • First launch of the day: your first launch each day (the day starts when you wake up, or at 4 AM, not at midnight) copies a list of short mindfulness reflections (instead of the calming list), waits a second, and then opens the roulette. You can do the same any time with the Roulette app's morning mode. Logging "Going to sleep" doesn't count as the day's first launch. If the same launch also triggers the frayed or doomscroll nudge, the calming list wins.
  • Doomscroll streak: launching feed-scrolling activities (short videos, social profiles, news feeds) 3 times in a row, with no other launch or break in between, does the same: the list is copied and the roulette opens. Another 3 in a row triggers it again.
  • Late night: between 10 PM and 5 AM (your local time) every stimulating launch adds 1.5× its normal points, including the app-switching penalty, because you are likely under-rested and should be heading to sleep. The browser doomscroll mode is boosted 2× instead. Calming and neutral launches (sleep, reading, meals and so on) are never boosted.
  • Browser scrolling modes: Chrome, Brave and DuckDuckGo have doomscroll (weight 8) and obsessivescroll (weight 12, the highest weight there is) modes. Both count towards the doomscroll streak.
  • Time: the level drops 0.3 points per idle minute, slowly, because settling after stimulation takes a while.
  • Break: works better the longer it lasts. A 15-minute break removes 60% of your level; shorter ones remove proportionally less (5 minutes removes 20%), so tapping it for a few seconds does almost nothing. It never resets to zero.
  • Rest counts up to 30 minutes: only the first 30 minutes of a break or an idle gap count, because the app can't see what you do on your phone outside it. After that the level holds still until your next action. Sleep (a gap of 5+ hours) is the exception and still brings the level right down.
  • Auto-break: if more than 20 minutes pass with no action (counted from when you came back from your last app), the next time you tap something the app records an inferred break with the same rule as a manual one, including the 30-minute limit. It cannot run in the background.
  • Glances: if you come back from an app within 10 seconds, half of that launch's points are handed back. App-switching and checking-loop penalties still count.
  • Waking up: a gap of 5+ hours is treated as sleep: it's logged as waking up, and your "day" in the data starts there instead of at midnight. Without a long sleep, the day changes at 4 AM.
  • App-switching penalty: opening 3 or more different apps within 5 minutes adds an extra 12 points, at most once every 5 minutes.
  • Checking loop: opening the same app 3 or more times within 10 minutes adds an extra 9 points, at most once every 10 minutes per app.
  • Range: always between 0 and 100. Your level is rebuilt from your own history each time you sign in.
  • Time since last break: counts from your last break; if you've never taken one, from your first logged action.

Assumptions to keep in mind

  • The weights are judgement calls. They are the creator's reading of what the book describes: intense sensory input, emotional processing, social pressure and interruptions raise arousal. The book gives no numbers, so every weight, the 1.5 multiplier, the decay rate and the 60% break relief are guesses to be tested.
  • The core hypothesis is untested: that what you launch adds up to how overstimulated you are. Your "Set my level" entries are how we find out whether that holds.
  • Time in an app is estimated from when you leave and come back to the launcher. If you come back in another way (a different tab, another device), it counts until your next action, or asks you after long gaps. If you never come back to close it (say you open the launcher again hours later), the default length is recorded at your next action.
  • Each app has a default activity, for example WhatsApp counts as group chat and YouTube as loud video. If you used it differently, pick a mode button.
  • Context modes (working, tired, bedtime) can't be detected, so they only count if you choose them.
  • This is not a medical or diagnostic tool. It isn't a test for being a highly sensitive person and gives no health advice.

Limits of a web app on a phone

  • Every button opens the app's website in a new tab. It can't open or track native apps.
  • It only counts launches made through this page. If you open Instagram yourself, the level won't see it. Use "Set my level" to correct for that.
  • It can't see what you do inside a site, or how long you stay.
  • If your browser blocks the new tab, the launch is still counted.

Your data & privacy

  • No real login. You get one of ten shared tester IDs. They aren't secret and there's no password.
  • What is logged, per action: time, your tester ID, what you tapped, the level before and after, band, minutes since last break, and the weight and points. Also your "Set my level" numbers and any feedback you send.
  • Who can see it: the creator can read everything. The database is open to anyone who finds the app's public key, so don't type personal or sensitive information into the feedback box.
  • Times use your phone's local clock for the date and hour.
  • Your history is stored on the server, so clearing your browser doesn't erase it.

Known issues

  • Use one tab or device at a time. The level is loaded when you sign in. If you have the app open in two places, they can show different levels, and an old tab can trigger a wrong auto-break. Reload the page to resync.
  • If you're offline, actions are saved on your phone and sent when you're back online.
  • After an update, you may need to refresh once to get the latest version.
